The best dashcam apps don't win because they have 50 features—they win because drivers trust that the recording will be there when something happens.

If I were designing Msafiri's Dashcam, I'd split it into three phases.

Phase 1 (MVP)

These are the features I wouldn't launch without.

1. Loop Recording ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

This is the foundation.

The app continuously records:

Minute 1
Minute 2
Minute 3
Minute 4
Minute 5

↓

Delete Minute 1

↓

Minute 6

The storage never fills up because old clips are overwritten.

Every dedicated dashcam does this.

2. One-Tap Lock Video ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Driver taps a big button.

Current recording becomes permanent.

Never deleted.

Useful when:

Near miss
Dangerous driver
Police interaction
Road rage
Accident ahead
3. Auto Lock After Impact ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Automatically save:

accident

A feature I haven't seen done particularly well

Because Msafiri already knows where speed cameras, police checkpoints, and hazards are, your dashcam could create event bookmarks automatically.

Imagine driving for two hours.

When you stop, the timeline shows:

08:15

📸 Speed Camera

08:29

👮 Police Checkpoint

08:41

🚧 Road Works

09:10

🚨 Accident

09:22

⚠ Hard Brake

09:35

📸 Speed Camera

09:42

🏁 Trip End

You don't need to scrub through two hours of footage. Tap any bookmark and you're instantly at that moment.

That's a natural fit for Msafiri because those events come from the same road intelligence you're already building.

If I had to pick just five features that would make Msafiri competitive from day one, they'd be:
Reliable loop recording with automatic storage management.
Automatic crash locking so critical footage is never lost.
GPS, speed, and timestamp overlays embedded in recordings.
Bluetooth/charging auto-start so drivers don't have to remember to begin recording.
Smart event timeline combining crash detection with Msafiri's alerts (speed cameras, checkpoints, hazards, and community reports).

That combination would deliver a polished experience while staying focused on what matters most: dependable recording and meaningful driving context, rather than a long list of rarely used settings.

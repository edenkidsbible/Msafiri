I am really at crossroads for one feature we have on Msafiri, and I neeed your advide:
I disabled users from reporting road alerts from locations far from them, like they cannot report an incident for a location that is 5km away or if they have not used the road recently in the past 3hrs. Is this a limiting factor, and we should just let them submit a road alert report for anywhere and any time?

Yes — I would change this. Your current 5 km / 3-hour restriction is probably too aggressive, especially if Msafiri's long-term goal is to become a community-powered road intelligence platform.

But I would not simply allow everyone to report anything, anywhere, at any time with equal credibility.

The better solution is:

Remove the hard restriction, but introduce a trust and freshness system behind the scenes.

That gives you the best of both worlds: much more community participation without turning Msafiri into a database full of stale or fabricated reports.

Why your current restriction is limiting

Imagine I'm driving on Thika Superhighway and I see a major accident.

I get home 10 km away.

Twenty minutes later, I realize:

"I should warn other Msafiri drivers."

But Msafiri says:

❌ You can't report this because you're more than 5 km away.

That's frustrating.

Even worse, someone may have personally witnessed an incident 2 hours ago, but can't report it because the 3-hour rule doesn't necessarily reflect whether the information is still useful.

And there are incidents where distance/time shouldn't matter much at all.

Example:

Pothole

I saw it yesterday.

It's probably still there.

Roadworks

I saw them this morning.

Likely still there.

Speed camera

I know exactly where the camera is.

My current location shouldn't matter.

Accident

Much more time-sensitive.

A report from 5 hours ago may be useless.

So you shouldn't have one universal rule for every incident type.

I'd build a "Report Confidence System"

When someone taps Report, let them report wherever they want.

But ask:

Where did you observe this?

📍 I'm here now

🕐 I saw it recently

📌 I know this location

That single distinction can become extremely valuable.

Then Msafiri calculates confidence

For example:

HIGH CONFIDENCE

User is currently at the location.

📍 Observed now

This is your strongest report.

GOOD CONFIDENCE

User was there 30 minutes ago.

🕐 Observed 30 min ago

Still valuable.

COMMUNITY TIP

User wasn't recently there but knows the location.

📌 Community tip

This shouldn't be treated the same as a live observation.

And this is where it gets really interesting.

Don't show users:

"This report is from 7 hours ago."

Instead, make freshness part of the alert itself.

For example:

Speed Camera

📸 Speed Camera

Confirmed · 12 min ago

Police

👮 Police Checkpoint

Reported 8 min ago

Pothole

🕳 Pothole

Reported today

Accident

💥 Accident

Reported 4 min ago

This creates much more trust.

But there's another important thing:
Different reports need different expiration periods.

This is where I'd make Msafiri considerably smarter.

Accidents

Very short-lived.

Maybe:

2–4 hours

Unless people reconfirm them.

Traffic

Extremely short-lived.

Maybe:

30–90 minutes

Police checkpoint

Potentially:

1–3 hours

But confirmation from multiple drivers extends it.

Alcoblow

Perhaps:

2–4 hours

Again, confirmations extend it.

Roadblock

Several hours.

Flooding

Could last:

6–24 hours

depending on the situation.

Roadworks

Several days/weeks.

Pothole

Potentially weeks or months.

Speed camera

Potentially permanent once sufficiently verified.

This means you can stop thinking:

"Is this report older than 3 hours?"

and start thinking:

"Is this report still relevant?"

That's a much better system.

I would also introduce "Confirm"

This could become one of Msafiri's most important community features.

Imagine I'm approaching a police checkpoint.

I receive:

👮 Police checkpoint ahead
Reported 12 min ago

Underneath:

Is it still there?

👍 Yes

👎 No

That is incredibly powerful.

Now your community is continuously cleaning your database.

And here's something I REALLY recommend

Don't allow someone to secretly choose an arbitrary location and pretend they just saw it.

If they're reporting remotely, make it explicit:

Where is the incident?

Then:

📍 Use my current location

or

🗺️ Choose location on map

If they choose the map:

This report wasn't observed at your current location.

Then:

When did you see it?
Just now
Within the last hour
Today
Earlier
I don't know

Now you have metadata that your backend can use.

Your backend can then calculate a Report Score

Something like:

Report confidence =

Location proximity
Time since observation
Reporter history
Number of confirmations
Number of conflicting reports
Incident type
Whether the reporter was actually driving that road
Whether dashcam evidence exists

You don't even have to expose this complicated calculation to the user.
